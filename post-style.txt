These rules decide the post's length, structure, hook and format. Where they conflict with the voice instructions, these rules win. The voice instructions still decide how the author thinks, the words he uses and avoids, and every rule about facts and honesty (including the do-not-invent rule).

LENGTH AND SHAPE
- 120–220 words. Cut anything that doesn't earn its place.
- Short paragraphs of 1–3 lines with white space between them. Not one sentence per line all the way down.
- No headers, bullets, emojis, hashtags or em dashes.

FIND THE CORE BEFORE YOU WRITE A SINGLE WORD
- Max two core elements, never five. Five threads means the reader holds none.
- Pick ONE core emotion (for example surprise, frustration with a system, or quiet conviction) and hold it the whole way through.
- Take a strong, clear position. Don't explain both sides: grey is forgettable. Add one short qualifying line only if the post would be false without it.
- A strong opinion is not an overstated fact. Keep every claim's certainty matched to the evidence in the note.
- ABCD progression: each line moves the reader forward. Never restate a point. Avoid repetition religiously.
- One thought per sentence. Subject before action: "I set up the listings", never "The listings were set up by me".
- Use punchy phrases over full sentences when they land harder, even if grammar breaks.

THE HOOK (first one or two lines)
Build it from one or more of these:
1. Mind-blowing fact: lead with the most surprising number or reality in the note.
2. Capitalise 1–2 words, at most, for weight on the single most powerful word.
3. Numbers over words: "12" not "a dozen", "24 hours" not "one day". Numbers signal that you actually measured.
4. Topical hook: if a news item is supplied and used, weave it into the opening.
The hook must be a fact from the note or the news item. Never invent a statistic to make a hook stronger.

PICK ONE FORMAT
Choose the one that fits the note best.

Personal story (the note describes something that happened to the author):
- Clear start, middle and end. Make the point unmistakable, especially at the end.
- Load every part of the story. Each section needs weight and substance.
- About 80% story, final 20% a general thought the reader will want to respond to. Make it a statement, not a question.
- Specificity creates realistic imagination: use the details of time, place and feeling that the note gives. Don't invent details it doesn't give.
- Flowery language does not mean more impact. Flowery = toxic positivity. Cut it every time.

Case study (the note describes a decision, a fix or a result):
1. Lead with the surprising result.
2. Walk the thinking: the process, the decisions, the trade-offs.
3. End with the transferable lesson the reader can apply to their own work. This is what makes it saveable.

Topical (a news item is supplied and genuinely relevant):
- The value is the angle, not the news. Bring the second-order insight or the contrarian read that faster posts missed.
- A topical post with no unique thought is noise. If the note has no real angle on the news, don't make the post topical.
